Summary

Over the Nov 16, 2021 to Nov 13, 2026 window the equal-weighted 15-stock portfolio returned 13.4% annualized versus 11.6% for SPY, so it outperformed the benchmark, with annualized volatility of 13.6% against SPY's 17.3%. Its CAPM beta of 0.64 (R² 0.67) makes it slightly less market-sensitive than the index: a 1% move in SPY came with roughly a 0.64% move in the portfolio. The CAPM alpha of 4.1% per year is not statistically significant. The worst drawdown was -15.3%: the portfolio peaked on Jan 04, 2022, bottomed on Jun 17, 2022, and regained the prior peak on Apr 17, 2023. Those dates match the 2022 rate-hike bear market. In the Fama-French 3-factor regression the alpha is not statistically significant; the SMB loading of -0.10 tilts the portfolio toward larger names and the HML loading of 0.22 toward value, relative to the market portfolio. With both alpha estimates this close to zero, the factor tilts account for the return difference versus SPY.

Methodology & Limitations

Price data
All price series are Yahoo Finance dividend- and split-adjusted closes pulled via yfinance. Returns therefore approximate a total-return series, with dividends reinvested at the ex-date close. The series exclude taxes and trading costs.
Survivorship and selection bias
The 15 names were chosen in hindsight from currently listed, successful US large caps. A portfolio formed five years ago by the same logic could have held names that later delisted or collapsed, so the statistics here overstate what a real ex-ante selection would have earned. The bias comes from the selection method, and better data cannot remove it.
Weighting and rebalancing
Equal weights, rebalanced daily: each day's portfolio return is the simple mean of constituent returns. Daily rebalancing is a modeling convenience that assumes zero transaction costs, taxes, and bid-ask spread, and a monthly- or never-rebalanced version would show modestly different numbers. Equal weighting is a deliberate, stated choice. It avoids concentration in the mega-caps, overweights the smaller names relative to a cap-weighted benchmark, and partly explains the deviations from SPY.
Risk-free rate
Risk-free rate: period-matching average of the FRED DGS3MO 3-month Treasury constant-maturity yield (4.02% annualized over this window), de-annualized geometrically to a daily rate for the Sharpe/Sortino calculations. DGS3MO is a quoted bond-equivalent yield, so it only approximates an investable cash rate; the difference is immaterial at this precision.
Factor data
Fama-French daily 3-factor returns from the Ken French Data Library. The FF market factor is the CRSP value-weighted total US market, which differs from SPY, so the FF market beta and the CAPM beta differ slightly by construction. FF excess returns use the library's own RF series. The library posts with a lag, so the last few trading days of the price window may be absent from the regression sample (n is reported in the table).
VaR methodology
Historical VaR is the empirical quantile of the realized daily return distribution over the full five-year window; it assumes the past distribution is representative. Parametric VaR assumes Gaussian daily returns, which understates tail risk for equity portfolios (realized kurtosis exceeds 3). Both are 1-day, unconditional measures computed over the full window, with no conditioning on current volatility.
Window sensitivity
Every statistic here is specific to this five-year window, which contains one major drawdown (2022) and a strong recovery. Shifting the window by even a year materially changes the Sharpe ratio, alpha, and drawdown statistics. None of these figures is a forecast of future performance.
